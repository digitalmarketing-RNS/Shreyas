01

Technical SEO

The layer that decides whether any of your other work counts. If Google can't crawl it, render it or load it fast enough, nothing else matters.

Technical SEO is unglamorous and it is where most stalled sites are actually stuck. A site can have excellent content and still rank nowhere because a stray noindex survived a staging deploy, or because faceted navigation has generated 40,000 near-duplicate URLs that are eating the crawl budget meant for your product pages.

I start every engagement here, because it changes what the rest of the plan should be. A full crawl, log-file review where server logs are available, Search Console coverage and Core Web Vitals field data from CrUX — real-user measurements, not a lab score from a fast laptop on office wifi.

What gets examined

  • Crawlability — robots.txt, crawl traps, orphan pages, internal redirect chains, crawl budget allocation across templates
  • Indexation — what's indexed that shouldn't be, what should be and isn't, canonical conflicts, index bloat from filters and parameters
  • Core Web Vitals — LCP, INP and CLS from CrUX field data, traced back to the specific render-blocking resource or layout shift causing it
  • JavaScript rendering — whether your content survives Google's render pass, which matters enormously for React and Vue sites
  • Site architecture — click depth, internal link equity distribution, whether your money pages are three clicks deep behind a blog
  • Security and delivery — HTTPS correctness, mixed content, redirect hygiene, hosting response times from Indian locations specifically

02

Keyword & intent research

Finding the queries with buyers behind them — and, just as important, identifying the ones you should ignore.

Most keyword research produces a spreadsheet of 2,000 terms sorted by volume. That's not research, it's a data export. The work is deciding which of those terms represent someone about to spend money, which represent someone doing homework, and which represent someone looking for a job at a company like yours.

That last category is a real trap in Bangalore. Search almost any 'freelancer' or 'services' term here and the results are half hiring intent, half job-seeking intent, with Indeed and Naukri ranking alongside businesses. Chasing that traffic brings volume that never converts and confuses Google about what your page is for.

How the shortlist gets built

  • Intent classification — every candidate keyword sorted into transactional, commercial-investigation, informational or navigational, by reading the actual SERP rather than trusting a tool's label
  • SERP analysis — who ranks, what page type Google rewards for that query, whether a map pack or AI Overview sits above the organic results and takes the clicks
  • Difficulty against your actual authority — not the generic score, but whether your domain can realistically compete for it this year
  • Gap analysis — terms your competitors rank for and you don't, filtered to the ones worth having
  • Cannibalisation check — where two of your pages compete for one query, which quietly caps both
  • Quick wins — positions 5–15, where you already have partial relevance and a focused push moves you into the top three inside a quarter

03

On-page SEO

Making each page the most obviously correct answer to the query it targets.

On-page SEO stopped being about keyword density roughly a decade ago, but a surprising amount of the advice online hasn't caught up. The modern question is comparative: does this page answer the query more completely than the four ranking above it, and is that obvious to a machine reading it?

In practice that's about coverage and structure. If every page ranking for your term addresses cost, timeline and comparison against the alternative, and yours addresses only features, you have a content gap that no amount of title-tag tuning will close.

What changes on the page

  • Titles and meta descriptions — written for the click, not just the crawler; measured against what's actually being displayed in the SERP rather than what you wrote
  • Heading structure — one H1, a hierarchy that reflects the actual argument, question-phrased H2s where a featured snippet or AI citation is available
  • Entity and topic coverage — the concepts Google expects to see on a page about this subject, identified from what's ranking
  • Internal linking — deliberate, with descriptive anchors, pushing authority toward the pages that make money rather than scattering it evenly
  • Snippet-shaped answers — self-contained 40–60 word blocks that can be lifted as a featured snippet or cited by an AI answer
  • Conversion elements — because a page that ranks and doesn't convert is a vanity metric with extra steps

04

Local SEO & Google Business Profile

For most Bangalore businesses this is the highest-return work available, because the map pack sits above every organic result.

When someone in Bangalore searches for a service near them, Google returns three businesses in a map pack before a single organic result. If you aren't in that block, position four organic is competing for what's left after three competitors have had first refusal.

The encouraging part is that the barrier is lower than people assume. In most Bangalore service categories, the businesses holding those three slots have somewhere between 15 and 40 reviews — not hundreds. This is winnable inside a quarter for most local businesses.

There is far more to say about this than fits here. The dedicated local SEO page covers the map pack, categories, review strategy and multi-location structure in full.

What local SEO involves

  • Primary category selection — the single strongest local ranking factor, and the one most businesses accept from an autocomplete suggestion without thinking
  • Profile completeness — services, attributes, hours, products, booking links; each completed field is a relevance signal
  • NAP consistency — your name, address and phone identical across every directory, because conflicting data is read as uncertainty
  • Review velocity and response — a steady flow, obtained within Google's policies, with every review answered
  • Local landing pages — genuinely distinct pages per area you serve, not a template with the locality swapped
  • Citations — Justdial, IndiaMART, Sulekha and the industry directories that still carry weight in India

05

Link building & digital PR

Earned authority from sources that would still make sense if Google disappeared tomorrow.

Links remain one of the strongest ranking signals and the easiest thing to get catastrophically wrong. The Indian market is full of vendors selling '500 backlinks for ₹5,000' — those are PBNs and link farms, they produce a brief lift, and they are precisely what Google's link spam systems are built to neutralise. Increasingly they don't even produce the brief lift.

I build links slowly and on purpose. Fewer placements, from sources with real audiences, which means the rankings survive the next update. If you need volume, I'm the wrong person, and I'd rather tell you now.

Where links come from

  • Digital PR — data, commentary or a genuinely newsworthy angle that trade and regional publications want to cover
  • Industry and association sites — relevant, often overlooked, and frequently the strongest links available to a B2B business
  • Resource and roundup placements — where your business genuinely belongs on the list
  • Local citations — the Indian directories that still pass value, done consistently rather than sprayed
  • Unlinked mention reclamation — you are already cited across the web without a link more often than you'd expect; this is usually the fastest available win
  • Toxic link audit — finding and disavowing what a previous vendor left behind, where it's actually causing harm

06

SEO content strategy

What to write, in what order — and more often than anyone expects, what to stop writing.

Most sites I audit have the opposite of a content problem. They have forty thin posts published because someone said to blog weekly, none of which rank, all of which dilute the site's topical focus and consume crawl budget.

Content strategy is mostly editorial judgement: identifying the eight pages that could genuinely rank, resourcing them properly, and pruning or consolidating the rest. Google's helpful-content systems assess sites holistically — a large volume of unhelpful pages can suppress the good ones.

How the plan gets built

  • Topic clusters — a substantial pillar page with genuine supporting depth around it, internally linked with intent
  • Content briefs — angle, structure, target query, required coverage, internal links, and what the competing pages miss
  • Pruning and consolidation — merging overlapping posts, redirecting the rest, removing what will never earn a click
  • Refresh cycles — updating pages that have slipped, which is consistently cheaper than writing new ones
  • E-E-A-T signals — real author attribution, credentials, citations, review dates; increasingly decisive in YMYL categories like health, legal and finance

07

Schema & structured data

Telling machines exactly what your business is, rather than hoping they infer it correctly.

Structured data is how you state facts about your business in a form that isn't open to interpretation. That has always helped with rich results. It matters considerably more now that AI systems are assembling answers from whatever they can parse confidently.

Worth being honest about the limits: Google retired FAQ rich results for most sites in 2026 and HowTo before that. Schema is not a ranking hack. What it does is remove ambiguity about who you are, what you sell, where you operate and who wrote this — and ambiguity is expensive.

What gets marked up

  • Person and Organization — the entity layer, with sameAs connecting every profile you own into one coherent identity
  • LocalBusiness / ProfessionalService — area served, hours, price range, geo coordinates
  • Product and Offer — price, availability, condition; still generates rich results for e-commerce
  • Article and author markup — a real, credentialed author rather than an empty byline
  • BreadcrumbList — cleaner SERP display and clearer hierarchy signals
  • Validation — everything tested in Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator, then re-checked in Search Console after deployment

08

AI search optimisation (GEO)

Being the source that AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity cite — which is a different target from ranking.

A meaningful share of informational queries now get answered without a click. For Bangalore businesses this matters less than the headlines suggest, because commercial and local intent still sends traffic and that's where the money is. But it has widened the job: the target is no longer only 'rank', it's also 'be the source the model reaches for'.

The honest position is that GEO is mostly good SEO with a specific emphasis. Clear, self-contained passages. Claims a machine can extract without ambiguity. Consistent entity data. Brand mentions across the web, because models weight being talked about, not just being linked to. Anyone selling GEO as a separate discipline with its own secret is selling something.

What changes for AI search

  • Citable passages — self-contained answers that make sense lifted out of the page, because that's how they'll be used
  • Entity consistency — the same name, role, location and credentials everywhere, so a model has no reason to doubt who you are
  • llms.txt — an emerging convention for telling AI crawlers what matters on your site; cheap to implement, plausibly useful
  • AI crawler access — checking you haven't accidentally blocked GPTBot, PerplexityBot or ClaudeBot in robots.txt, which is more common than you'd think
  • Brand mention building — unlinked mentions in relevant contexts, which feed the models' sense of who is notable in a category
  • Answer monitoring — tracking whether you're being cited for your core queries, and what gets cited instead when you aren't

Questions

About scope and fit

Which of these services does my business actually need?

Usually three of them, not all eight. The audit decides: a site with crawl and speed problems needs technical work before content; a site with good pages nobody links to needs authority; a single-location service business often needs local SEO and almost nothing else. Paying for all eight at once is how retainers get expensive without getting effective.

Do you do SEO content writing yourself?

I write the briefs and the commercially important pages myself. For volume content I work with writers I've used for years, and I edit everything before it publishes. I don't publish AI-generated drafts unedited — Google's helpful-content systems are good at spotting it and the rankings don't hold.

Can you fix a Google penalty or a traffic drop after a core update?

Yes, and it starts with diagnosis rather than action. A manual action shows in Search Console and has a defined recovery path. An algorithmic drop doesn't — it needs identifying which update hit you, what it targeted, and whether your site has that problem. Guessing wastes months, which is why most recovery attempts fail.

Do you handle e-commerce SEO?

Yes, for considered-purchase catalogues. E-commerce SEO is mostly a structural problem: faceted navigation creating index bloat, duplicate product descriptions, category pages that don't target anything, and pagination handled badly. The content work comes after that's fixed.

What about international or multi-city SEO?

Multi-city needs genuinely distinct location pages, not a template with the city name swapped — Google has been demoting those for years. International needs correct hreflang, which is where most implementations break. Both are Custom-plan work because the structure decisions matter more than the ongoing effort.
