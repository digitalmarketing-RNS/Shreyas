Diagnosis

Your site ranks and the phone still isn't ringing. Here's why.

BY SHREYAS V PATIL · SEO FREELANCER, BANGALORE · 2 SEPTEMBER 2026 · 9 MIN READ

This is the single most common problem I'm brought in to fix. The rankings are fine. Analytics shows the traffic. The phone does not ring. Here are the six causes, in the order I check them — because the first two account for most cases and cost nothing to rule out.

1. You're ranking for the wrong half of the intent

Indian SERPs split intent more aggressively than most markets, and Bangalore is a bad offender. Search almost any "service in Bangalore" term and the results mix businesses offering the service with Indeed and Naukri listings for people who want a job doing it. The People Also Ask box tells you the same story — half hiring questions, half "how do I become a…" questions.

If your page is picking up the second group, you get traffic, a decent time-on-page, and no enquiries. Ever. Open Search Console, sort your queries by impressions, and read the actual terms rather than the totals. If a meaningful share are job-seeker phrasings, that's your answer and no amount of CRO will fix it.

The fix is to stop feeding it. Don't write the "how to become" content. Answer the hiring questions — what it costs, how to choose, what's included, how long it takes — and let the other traffic go to Naukri where it belongs.

2. You're not in the map pack, so the high-intent clicks never reach you

For local-intent searches in Bangalore, three businesses appear in a map block above every organic result, with Call and Directions buttons attached. Someone tapping Call from that block has already decided. Someone scrolling past it to your organic result at position four is, by definition, still comparing.

So you can rank organically, get respectable traffic, and still lose the enquiries — because the three businesses above you have taken the visitors who were ready to buy and left you the ones who are browsing.

This is usually the cheapest problem on the list to fix. In most Bangalore service categories the map-pack incumbents have between 15 and 40 reviews, which is a quarter's work, not a year's. The local SEO page covers the sequence in detail.

3. Your page ranks for a query it doesn't actually answer

Someone searches "company registration cost in Bangalore". They land on a page that explains what company registration is, why it's important, and invites them to contact you for a quote. They wanted a number. They leave and find a competitor who gave them one.

This is an enormously common failure and it hides well, because the page ranks. Google decided it was relevant. The visitor decided it wasn't useful. The gap between those two judgements is where your enquiries are disappearing.

Check it directly: for your top ten landing pages by traffic, write down the query each one ranks for, then read the page as someone with that exact question. If the answer isn't in the first screen, that's the problem. This is also why I publish actual prices — a visitor who learns the number and leaves was never going to convert, and a visitor who learns it and stays is worth ten of the other kind.

4. Nothing on the page is a next step

A phone number in the header is not a call to action. It's contact information. There's a real difference between "here is how to reach us if you decide to" and "here is the obvious next thing to do."

What tends to work for Indian local businesses, in rough order: a WhatsApp link with a pre-filled message, because it's the lowest-commitment channel there is and people will use it at 11pm; a click-to-call number that's actually answered; a short form that asks three things rather than nine; and a specific offer — "send me your site and I'll tell you what's wrong with it" beats "get in touch" because it describes what happens next.

Also check the obvious: does your form work on a phone? Does it send anywhere? I have genuinely found broken contact forms on sites where the owner was convinced SEO had stopped working. Test it yourself from a mobile, today.

5. The page loads slowly enough that people leave first

Most Bangalore traffic is mobile, a lot of it on mobile data of varying quality. A page that takes six seconds to become usable loses a large share of visitors before they've read a word — and those visitors register in Analytics as a session, which is why your traffic numbers look fine.

Check the field data in PageSpeed Insights, not the lab score. The lab score is your page on a fast machine. Field data is real Bangalore users on real phones, which is the only number that matters here.

6. You're getting enquiries and not counting them

Worth ruling out before you spend money on the other five. WhatsApp messages, direct calls, and walk-ins prompted by a Google search frequently go uncounted, so the business concludes SEO isn't working while it quietly is.

Ask every new enquiry how they found you, for one month. Set up GA4 events on your calls and WhatsApp clicks. Check Google Business Profile Insights for call and direction requests — those are search-driven enquiries that never touch your website at all and therefore never appear in Analytics.

The order to work through it

Start with 6, because it's free and it happens more than you'd think. Then 2, because it's the cheapest genuine fix. Then 1, because if the intent is wrong nothing downstream will help. Then 3, 4 and 5, which are page-level work.

What you should not do is write more content. Adding pages to a site that already ranks and doesn't convert produces more traffic that doesn't convert, and dilutes the site's topical focus while you're at it. Diagnose first.
