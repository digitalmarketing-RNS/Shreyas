AI Overviews and Indian local search: what actually changed

BY SHREYAS V PATIL · SEO FREELANCER, BANGALORE · 18 AUGUST 2026 · 8 MIN READ

Every SEO conference this year has been about AI search, most of it pitched at a level of alarm that doesn't match what I'm seeing in client Search Console data. Here is what has actually moved for Indian businesses, what hasn't, and the small number of things worth doing about it.

What genuinely changed

Informational queries lost clicks. This part is real and it's significant. When someone searches "what documents are needed for GST registration", an AI Overview now answers it above the results. The user gets what they came for and doesn't click. If your traffic was built on explainer content, you have felt this.

The zero-click share went up. It was already high — featured snippets and knowledge panels have been eating informational traffic for years. AI answers accelerated a trend rather than starting one.

Citation became a distinct target. AI Overviews link to sources. Being one of those sources drives traffic and, more importantly, puts your brand inside the answer itself. That's a different optimisation goal from ranking, and it rewards slightly different things.

What didn't change, and this is the part that matters

Commercial intent still sends traffic. Someone searching "SEO freelancer in Bangalore" or "company registration in Pune" is trying to hire someone. An AI summary can describe what an SEO freelancer does; it cannot be one. Those searchers still click through, still compare, still enquire. For most Bangalore businesses this is where the revenue was already coming from.

Local intent is largely untouched. "Dentist near me" returns a map pack. "Restaurants in Indiranagar" returns a map pack. Google has no incentive to replace local results with a text answer, because the map pack is more useful and better monetised. If your business depends on local search, very little has changed for you.

Transactional queries still convert. Nobody buys from a summary. They read it, then go and look at the actual businesses.

So the honest summary for a Bangalore local business: you probably lost some blog traffic that was never producing enquiries anyway, and your commercial and local visibility is broadly intact. That is a much less dramatic story than the conference circuit is telling, and it matches what the data shows.

The things actually worth doing

Write passages that survive being lifted out

AI systems extract chunks. A paragraph that only makes sense after three paragraphs of preamble won't get used. A self-contained 40 to 60 word block that answers one question completely will. This is the same discipline that wins featured snippets, so you're not doing new work — you're doing existing work more deliberately.

Make your claims specific enough to cite

"SEO takes time" is unusable. "Local SEO typically shows movement in 30 to 90 days, while competitive organic terms take four to six months" is citable, because it's a definite claim a model can attribute. Vagueness was always weak writing; it's now also invisible.

Keep your entity data consistent

Same name, same role, same location, same credentials across your site, LinkedIn, your Google Business Profile and every directory. Models assemble a picture of who you are from whatever they can find. Contradictions make them less confident, and less confident means less likely to cite you.

This is what Person and Organization schema with proper sameAs links is for. It was always good practice. It now has a second consumer.

Check you haven't blocked the AI crawlers

More common than you'd expect. Somebody adds a blanket Disallow for GPTBot, PerplexityBot or ClaudeBot — often on generic advice about "protecting content" — and then wonders why the brand never appears in AI answers. Decide deliberately. For most businesses trying to be found, blocking them is self-defeating.

Add an llms.txt, cheaply

An emerging convention: a plain-text file at your root pointing AI crawlers at what matters. Adoption is uneven and nobody should promise you results from it. It takes fifteen minutes and the downside is zero. Mine is here if you want a template.

Build mentions, not just links

Models weight being talked about. An unlinked mention in a relevant industry article contributes to a model's sense of who matters in a category in a way that a link in a directory doesn't. Digital PR was already the better half of link building; this widens the gap.

What to ignore

Anyone selling "GEO" as a separate discipline with proprietary techniques. There is no secret. It's clear writing, consistent entity data, structured markup and genuine authority — which is what good SEO has been for a decade. The emphasis has shifted; the work hasn't changed nearly as much as the pricing of it suggests.

Also ignore anyone telling you SEO is dead. It's been dead annually since roughly 2011. Meanwhile a client of mine has held #1 for their core commercial keywords for over two years across every update in that window, and the enquiries keep arriving.
