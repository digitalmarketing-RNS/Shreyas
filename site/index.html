01 · Who you'd be hiring

Ten years of Indian SERPs, not ten years of slide decks

I started in SEO when the Indian search market was still mostly a numbers game — buy enough links, stuff enough keywords. That stopped working, and most of the people doing it left. I stayed, because the part that interested me was never the trick. It was the diagnosis: why does this page not rank when the one above it is worse?

Today I work as an SEO freelancer in Bangalore with clients across Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana and Tamil Nadu — a tile manufacturer, an engineering college, a company-registration firm, an industrial crane maker, a laundry chain, a property developer. Different industries, same job: find the queries with money behind them, and make the site the most obviously correct answer to them.

Bangalore matters to how I work. Search here is multilingual and messy — people type "pg near manyata tech park" and "best dentist hsr layout" and half of it in transliterated Kannada. Ranking in this city means understanding that the searcher in Whitefield and the searcher in Jayanagar are looking for different things with the same words.

You'll deal with me. Not an account manager, not a pod, not a junior executive who joined last month. That's the entire reason clients leave agencies for freelancers, and it's the reason my retention rate is what it is.

04 · How an engagement runs

The first thirty days, specifically

You should know what happens before you pay for it. This is the actual sequence, and the reason month one looks slow — the foundation work is what makes months three to six move.

Week 1 · Discovery

Find out what's actually wrong

Full technical crawl, Search Console history, competitor gap analysis, and a conversation about which enquiries you actually want more of. Half the time the problem isn't rankings — it's that the pages ranking are the wrong pages.

Week 1 · Plan

A roadmap ordered by return, not by effort

Quick-win keywords first — the ones sitting at positions 5–15 where a real push moves them into the top three inside a quarter. Structural work runs underneath it. You see the priority order and can argue with it.

Weeks 2–3 · Execution

Technical fixes and on-page rebuild

Speed, crawl errors, schema, internal linking, titles and content on the priority pages. This is the part that requires developer access or a CMS login; I'll work with whoever maintains your site, or directly if that's simpler.

Week 4 · Authority

Links and citations start

Outreach begins once the pages are worth linking to — doing it in the other order wastes the placements. For local businesses, Google Business Profile and citation cleanup runs in this window too.

Monthly · Ongoing

Report, read it together, adjust

A monthly call over your own Search Console and GA4 — not a branded PDF. Position changes, what caused them, what's next, and what didn't work. Honest reporting is the only kind that survives two years, which is how long the average client stays.

10 · Questions

Questions people ask before hiring an SEO freelancer

The ones that come up on almost every first call, answered the way I'd answer them on the call.

What does an SEO freelancer in Bangalore actually do?

An SEO freelancer improves how visible your website is in Google's results, working directly with you rather than through an agency. The work covers technical SEO, on-page optimisation, content strategy, local SEO and link building. In Bangalore it usually leans heavily on local search, because most enquiries here start with a "near me" or area-specific query.

How much does an SEO freelancer charge in Bangalore?

Most competent freelance SEOs in Bangalore charge between ₹10,000 and ₹25,000 per month. My plans start at ₹4,000 for Google Business Profile work alone and ₹12,000 for full SEO. Agencies typically quote ₹30,000 to ₹75,000+ for comparable scope, with the difference covering overheads rather than extra work on your site.

Rates below about ₹5,000 for full SEO usually mean automated reports and bought links. That is how sites get penalised. The full pricing page explains what actually moves the number.

How long does SEO take to show results?

Expect early movement in 60 to 90 days and meaningful commercial results in four to six months. Local SEO and Google Business Profile work can move faster — sometimes inside 30 days — because the map pack responds more quickly than organic rankings.

Anyone promising page one in 30 days for a competitive keyword is describing something other than SEO. The variables are your site's current state, how contested your keywords are, and how quickly changes actually get deployed.

Can you guarantee a #1 ranking on Google?

No, and neither can anyone else. Google's guidelines explicitly warn against SEOs who guarantee rankings. What I can commit to is the process, the reporting and the honesty: you see your own Search Console data every month, including the months where something didn't work.

What I have done is take named clients to #1 for named commercial keywords and hold those positions for years. That's on the case studies page, with the verification source for each.

Is hiring a freelancer riskier than hiring an agency?

In one specific way, yes: an agency has continuity if a person leaves, and a freelancer doesn't. That's a real risk and you should price it in. Practically, it's managed by keeping everything in your accounts — your Search Console, your Analytics, your hosting, your GBP — so nothing is locked behind me.

Against that, you avoid the more common risk: paying agency rates for work done by someone with eighteen months' experience, discovered only after six contracted months.

Do I need to be in Bangalore to work with you?

No. Most of my clients I meet on calls, and several are in Pune, Hyderabad and Chennai. Being Bangalore-based matters when your customers are here — local search behaviour, area-level competition and the language mix in queries are things I know first-hand rather than from a tool.

What do you need from me to start?

Access to Google Search Console and Google Analytics (or permission to set them up), your website's CMS or a developer contact, and your Google Business Profile if you have one. Plus thirty minutes to tell me which enquiries actually make you money — that conversation usually changes the keyword strategy more than any tool does.

Do you work on a contract or lock-in?

No lock-in. Month to month, and you can stop whenever you like. My average client stays over two years anyway, which I'd argue is a better indicator than a contract would be. Contracts mostly protect the agency from its own churn.

What kind of businesses do you work best with?

Businesses where one enquiry is worth something — manufacturers, professional services, education, healthcare, real estate, B2B, considered-purchase e-commerce. The common factor is a real product and a reason to be chosen; SEO amplifies that, it doesn't invent it.

I'm a poor fit for anything needing traffic volume at any quality, or anyone wanting to rank a page that doesn't deserve to.

How is AI search changing SEO in 2026?

AI Overviews, ChatGPT search and Perplexity now answer a large share of informational queries without a click. Commercial and local intent still drive traffic, which is where most Bangalore businesses actually make money — but the optimisation target has widened from "rank" to "be the source that gets cited".

In practice that means clearer, self-contained passages, well-structured entity data, and brand mentions across the web. It's an addition to good SEO, not a replacement for it.

Will you work with my existing developer or agency?

Yes, and often that's the cleanest setup — I diagnose and specify, your developer ships. I'll write tickets in whatever detail your team needs. If you already have an agency doing paid media, I'll work alongside them rather than trying to take the account.
