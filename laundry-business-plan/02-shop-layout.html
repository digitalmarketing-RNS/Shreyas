Remove first

Take out the old black grilled counter in the back room. It stands on the right wall, where the two stacks go.

Why it's laid out this way

  • Water stays behind the white doorThe back room already has water points on the rear wall, a high window and the toilet's drain, and its old tiles need replacing anyway. Re-tile it once, sloping to a floor trap, and the new front-room tiles never get wet.
  • Stacking saves wall spaceTwo stacks use about 5 ft of the right wall. Three machines side by side would need 7–8 ft and block the rear wall, where the sink and bins go.
  • Dirty and clean only cross at the doorTagged bags go up the left side to the rack by the door. Clean clothes come out the same door to the folding table. Dirty bags never go on the folding table.
  • Ironing sits at the frontThe road can see it, the open shutter carries the steam and heat out, and the rail and counter are a few steps away.
  • Both shelves sit near the counterFolded orders go on the left wall behind the counter, and ironed orders hang on the right wall, so staff can hand any order over in seconds.
  • One dryer limits your daily volumeA 10 kg dryer finishes about 10 loads (around 80 kg) in a 12-hour day. Two washers can wash more than that, which is why Dryer 2's spot and wiring are ready from day one.

What the letters mark

  1. AWater valves at 1.1 m, one per washer, behind the machines
  2. B50 mm drain standpipe, 0.6–0.9 m high, behind each washer
  3. C100 mm dryer vent through a metal plate in the window's top pane. Keep it under 3 m long with no more than 2 bends.
  4. DIsolators at about 2.2 m, above splash height: 16 A per washer, 32 A per dryer. Dryer 2's isolator is wired and capped.
  5. EMachine DB at 1.5 m, away from water

Stacking

Buy the stacking kit made for your models, and stand each stack on anti-vibration pads. Loading the top dryer at about chest height is normal in laundromats.

That adds up to about 17 kW connected now and about 23 kW once Dryer 2 arrives, so you need a 3-phase supply. Put the two dryers on different phases. Add a 30 mA RCCB for the wash room, a dedicated earth pit, and 5000–6500 K LED panels over the counter and folding table so stains are easy to see.

Plumber

  • SupplyTank → sediment filter → softener → 20 mm line along the right wall at 1.1 m. One quarter-turn valve per washer and one for the sink.
  • PressureCheck the minimum water pressure in each washer's manual. Water flowing down from a tank only a few metres up is often too weak for front-loaders. Add a small booster pump if the machines fill slowly.
  • DrainEach washer pumps into a 50 mm standpipe. A 75 mm line under the new tiles runs to the floor trap and on to the toilet drain.
  • FloorAnti-skid tiles in the back room, sloped about 1 in 100 towards the floor trap, with 150 mm tiled skirting.
  • SinkSteel utility sink, about 600 × 500 mm and 300 mm deep, at the existing water points
  • Vent + fan100 mm aluminium dryer duct and a 300 mm exhaust fan, both through the window. Clean the dryer's lint filter after every load.

Check these before work starts

  • Measure both rooms, the ceiling height, and the window's sill and top. This plan assumes a 12 × 16 ft front room and a 12 × 8 ft back room.
  • Measure the white door's clear width. Commercial machines are about 700 mm wide. If the opening is under about 750 mm, take the door off its hinges to bring them in.
  • Measure the back room's right wall. The two stacks need about 5 ft of it, starting about 2 ft from the rear wall. If it's shorter, put Stack 2 against the rear wall in place of the sorting bins.
  • Confirm the rear-wall water points work, and find where the toilet drain runs.
  • Get the landlord's written permission to remove the black counter, re-tile, fit a vent plate in the window pane and install the exhaust fan.
  • Apply to BESCOM for a 3-phase connection before you order the machines.
