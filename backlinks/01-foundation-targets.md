## Tier A — universal core

Claim these regardless of business type.

| Target | DA (guide, unverified) | Link type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | 100 | n/a (entity) | Not a backlink — an entity/knowledge signal. Only if you have a real address or service area. Skip for pure-remote SaaS |
| LinkedIn Company Page | 98 | no-follow | No link equity, high trust + entity signal. Table stakes |
| GitHub (org or profile) | 96 | no-follow (bio links) | Strong signal for dev-facing products; near-useless otherwise |
| Crunchbase | 91 | no-follow (free tier) | Entity/knowledge-graph value. Do-follow historically gated behind paid tiers — verify |
| Trustpilot | 93 | no-follow | Review signal. Real value is social proof + brand SERP, not equity |
| Wikidata | — | no-follow | **Not in the guide, worth adding.** Entity presence correlates strongly with AI-search citation. Free, high leverage, easy to get wrong — needs genuine notability |

Honest read: **most Tier A links are no-follow.** Their value is entity
definition, brand SERP control and AI-citability — not PageRank. The guide is
right that they're the floor; they are also the least likely to move a ranking.
Claim them because they're cheap and expected, not because they'll climb.

### Dev tool / open source
GitHub org (properly filled), StackShare, AlternativeTo, Product Hunt,
Libraries.io, plus the package registry for your ecosystem (npm / PyPI / crates.io)
— registry profile links are legitimate and often overlooked.

### Local service
Google Business Profile (primary), Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp,
BBB, local Chamber of Commerce, plus the three data aggregators (Data Axle,
Foursquare, Neustar/TransUnion) for downstream distribution. **NAP consistency is
the whole game here** — one canonical address string, byte-identical everywhere.

### Ecommerce
Trustpilot, Google Merchant Center, marketplace presence, niche review sites for
the vertical. Product schema correctness matters more than directory count.

### Agency / services
Clutch, G2 (services categories), LinkedIn, industry association directories,
local chamber. Case-study-driven — Clutch reviews require client participation,
so start that outreach early.

## Submission asset checklist

Almost every directory asks for the same pack. Prepare **one canonical version**
of each and reuse it verbatim — inconsistency across listings is the classic
campaign-killer.

- [ ] **Exact legal/brand name** — one string, never varied
- [ ] **Canonical URL** — decide `https://` + www-or-not, then never deviate
- [ ] **Short description** — ~50 words
- [ ] **Long description** — 150–500 words
- [ ] **One-liner** — under 100 characters, for tight fields
- [ ] **Logo** — square, ≥512px, transparent PNG
- [ ] **Screenshots** — 2–3, 1280×800 or larger
- [ ] **Category** — primary + secondary
- [ ] **Pricing** — model + entry price, or "free"
- [ ] **Founded date**, **team size**, **location**
- [ ] **Social profiles** — the `sameAs` set, consistent across every listing
- [ ] **Contact email** — one that survives; verification mail lands here

I draft the descriptions in your voice via `humanizer` once the proof point and
one-liner arrive. You supply logo and screenshots — I can't produce those.

## Sequencing

Directory research converges on this cadence, and it matches the skill's
velocity red-flags (a sudden link spike on a brand-new domain reads as
manipulation):

1. **Days 1–7** — Tier A + the must-have Tier B for your type
2. **Weeks 2–8** — 2–3 niche directories per week
3. **Day 90 target** — 30–50 unique referring domains

Steady beats bulk. The point isn't the count; it's that the profile grows the
way a real business's profile grows.
