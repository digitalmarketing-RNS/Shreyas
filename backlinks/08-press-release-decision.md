# Decision: paid press release distribution

**Recommendation: no, not now.** Redirect the budget into original research.

---

## Why not

**1. The links carry no ranking credit by design.**

Google's spam policy treats a paid placement as requiring `rel="sponsored"` or
`rel="nofollow"`. Press release distribution is a paid placement. A distributed
release with followed, keyword-anchored links to your own site is the textbook
example Google names in its link schemes documentation. Compliant releases are
nofollow, which means no PageRank. Non-compliant ones are a policy violation.

There is no version of paid PR distribution that legitimately passes ranking
credit. You are choosing between a link that does nothing and a link that is a
violation.

**2. You already have one, and it is doing the work a second would do.**

The openpr.com release is titled "EEAT Minds Emerges As The Best Digital
Marketing Freelancer In India Revolutionizing Business Growth with Comprehensive
360 Digital Solutions."

One paid release declaring yourself the best is unremarkable. Two starts to look
like a pattern, and the pattern is legible to anyone who checks.

**3. Your profile cannot afford it right now.**

Current and planned referring domains break down as: competitor freelancer
roundups (reciprocal), business directories (mostly nofollow), and one paid
press release. Adding a second paid release means the profile contains **zero
earned editorial links**. That is the actual problem, and PR does not solve it.

The backlink-quality reference lists press release syndication networks at #23,
under patterns to monitor. One is noise. A habit is a signal.

**4. You sell SEO, so your link profile is your portfolio.**

This is the argument that should decide it. A prospect evaluating you, or a
journalist deciding whether to quote you, can see your backlink profile with a
free tool in thirty seconds. Paid PR plus reciprocal listings is what a client
hires you to fix. It is a bad look in a way it would not be for a plumber.

---

## When a press release would be right

Only when there is genuine news, meaning something a journalist would cover
without being paid to:

- A funding round or acquisition
- A notable hire or partnership
- An award from an independent body, not a pay-to-enter one
- **Original research or a dataset nobody else has**
- A genuine milestone with a real number attached

"We exist and we are good at marketing" is not news, and a distribution service
will happily take money to publish it anyway.

---

## The better version of this instinct

The instinct behind the press release is right: you want authority and coverage.
The mechanism is wrong. Do this instead.

**Publish original research on Bangalore small business marketing.**

Survey 100 to 150 Bangalore SMBs on what they spend on marketing, what they
tried, what worked, and what they regret. A Google Form, your existing client
network, a few local business WhatsApp groups, and two weeks.

Why this beats a press release on every axis:

| | Paid press release | Original research |
|---|---|---|
| Link type | nofollow or sponsored | Followed editorial links from anyone citing it |
| Who links | Syndication sites only | Journalists, bloggers, other agencies, forever |
| Cost | Distribution fee | Mostly your time |
| Decay | One-off, dead in a week | Compounds, gets cited for years |
| Credibility | Self-declared | Evidenced |
| AI citation | Minimal | Original data is among the most cited content types |

It also fixes the problem underneath everything else in this campaign: you
currently have no proof point that belongs to you. "66+ brands" is a claim.
"We surveyed 127 Bangalore businesses and 61% could not name their own top
traffic source" is a fact that other people will repeat while crediting you.

That is also what makes Source of Sources work. Journalists do not want a
freelancer's opinion. They want someone holding data.

**And then, if you want, that research is a legitimate press release.** Real
news, worth distributing, and the coverage it earns is followed editorial rather
than syndicated nofollow.

---

## If you go ahead anyway

Your call, and it is defensible if the goal is brand visibility rather than
links. In that case:

- Use it for **brand mentions**, not links. Unlinked brand mentions correlate
  more strongly with AI-search citation than backlinks do, so the release still
  has value on that axis
- Keep anchor text branded. "EEAT Minds" not "best digital marketing freelancer
  in Bangalore." Optimised anchors in a distributed release is the specific thing
  Google's link schemes doc names
- Accept nofollow without trying to negotiate followed links
- **One release, then stop.** Do not build a cadence
- Do not repeat the superlative framing of the first one
